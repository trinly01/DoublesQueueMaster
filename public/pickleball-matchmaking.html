Six ways to build a match

  • Pro Pick — drafts top- or bottom-rated players first
  • Casual — always the most even teams possible
  • Competitive — close games with partner/opponent variety
  • Standard — balanced with rotation mixing
  • Social — prioritizes new matchups over tight balance
  • Manual Match — pick 4 players, balance teams, shuffle, or swap; a live indicator shows each side's chance to win

Ratings that actually mean something

Every player starts at 1450. Wins and losses move the rating based on opponent strength and score margin — a blowout counts more than a squeaker. In doubles, the stronger partner takes a modestly larger share of the result, so a strong player can't pull a weak partner's rating up unfairly.

Levels: Beginner (<1400), Intermediate (1400+), Advanced (1700+), Expert (1900+), Pro (2100+).

Fixed Duo and Avoid — the social glue

Some pairs are inseparable; some shouldn't share a court. Fixed Duo welds two players into one draftable unit. Avoid lists keep chosen players off the same team. Both are respected by every matchmaking mode.

Proof it works

Leaderboards, Best Duo rankings, partner and rival records, and clutch stats (games decided by 2 points or less) — all computed from the matches your session actually ran.
