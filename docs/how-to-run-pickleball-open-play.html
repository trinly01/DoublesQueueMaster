How to run pickleball open play

The organizer's guide: pick a rotation format, keep the line honest, balance the games, and handle the drop-ins — without becoming the night's referee.

1. Pick your rotation format

Single FIFO line

Everyone joins one queue; the next four (or two) play when a court opens. Simplest to explain, hardest to argue with. Best for mixed-level social nights.

Winners / losers split

Winners re-enter a winners queue; losers a losers queue. Winners face winners — competitive nights love it, but make re-entry rules explicit or the losers queue stalls.

Paddle stack

The physical default: players stack paddles, the next four go on. Zero setup — and zero defense against bumped paddles, ambiguous order, and the same foursome re-stacking together.

2. Keep the line honest

  • Fix each player's position at check-in — visible order kills disputes
  • Decide the rule for winners re-entering before play starts
  • Have a sit-out mechanic: players who skip a turn shouldn't lose their night
  • Lock players who need a break without letting them get drafted

3. Balance the games

Lopsided matches are the #1 reason open play attendance drops. Rate your players (even roughly), split drafted groups into even teams, and rotate partners so the same pair doesn't dominate — or get dominated — all night. A dedicated duo that insists on playing together should stay together — force it in the team split, not at the queue.

4. Handle drop-ins and leavers

Latecomers join the back of the queue — no exceptions. Leavers get checked out so they stop absorbing draft picks. Both should take one tap, not a reshuffle of the board.

5. Close the loop

If scores matter to your group, record them. Ratings that persist across sessions turn casual open play into a ladder — and if your club reports to DUPR, exporting a CSV beats typing results at midnight.
